\section*{Overview of the revision}

Following Reviewer 4's review of our code, the policy comparisons now come from recorded PPO and SAC experiments, replacing the benchmark stubs used for the submitted comparisons. The revision also provides more detailed task implementations and outcome definitions, and evaluates proposals from three vision-language models. The response to Reviewer 4 details these modifications, and we thank Reviewer 4 for strengthening the work.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
The main changes are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.3em]
\item Recorded PPO and SAC policies (ten seeds, held-out evaluation), a matched
QP control, 200 held-out procedural geometries and specification controls, each changing one factor where possible.
\item A frozen-policy study: with the admissible set fixed, rescaling one
command scale in the projection metric lowers PPO SCREW safe completion from
60.0\% to as low as 20.0\%, while every forwarded command stays admissible.
\item Four representation and feasibility properties, proved as propositions in the Supplementary Information, replace the submitted ISS theorem; policy adaptation is reported as an empirical finding.
\item Actual VLM outputs: of 257 grounded command pairs from 273 decisions on
91 PCB images, TRiX prevents all 31 recorded constraint-violation cases and
introduces none.
\item Physical intervention evidence on a six-degree-of-freedom Seeed reBot
B601-RS arm (three video-backed trials, three logged repetitions).
\item A prospectively declared shifted-reset evaluation: in SNAP's
outside-support shell, the TRiX and static policy-and-governor systems, trained separately, reach 59.9\% and 3.5\% safe completion, yet fractures occur in 401 and 468 of 1,000 episodes per arm.

\item Mapped source and evidence, seed-level uncertainty, documented provenance, and a restructured manuscript with a redrawn Figure~1.
\end{enumerate}
