\section{Admissibility definitions and scope}

The revised analysis drops the nominal-controller stability and
bounded-neural-error assumptions of the submitted ISS argument.

\paragraph{Declared task context.}
Context-conditioned results assume the governor receives the task context $c$
of $\mathcal{A}(x,c)$, such as primitive identity, phase or
task-frame orientation. Context-limited runtime arms are identified by the
observation fields their bounds use, not necessarily every field of a shared
task observation (\suppsection{S4}).

\paragraph{Projection well-posedness.}
Projection requires a nonempty closed admissible set for a Euclidean minimizer
to exist; convexity makes it unique. Non-projectable symbolic
violations return REJECT.

\paragraph{Specification validity.}
Membership in $\mathcal{A}(x,c)$ means the \emph{modeled} command-level
constraint holds, not that its threshold, margin, coordinate transform or
physical model is correct, so specification validity is evaluated
separately from solver correctness, a distinction central to the BATTERY
matched-specification experiment and PCB margin audit.

\paragraph{Execution scope.}
The analytical results concern only command admissibility and representation,
not closed-loop stability, forward invariance of an unmodeled plant, or
robustness to arbitrary state-estimation, tracking, perception or contact-model
error.


\section{Reference projection operators}

The revised benchmark defines explicit admissible action sets and analytical
operators that enforce them; not every task constraint is an equality manifold.


\subsection{Helical projection (SCREW)}

SCREW v3 uses normalized command
$a=(a_v,a_\omega,a_r)$, with
$v_z^c=S_v a_v$, $\omega_z^c=S_\omega a_\omega$,
$F_r^c=S_r a_r$ and constants
$S_v=0.05$ m\,s$^{-1}$, $S_\omega=60$ rad\,s$^{-1}$,
$S_r=40$ N, $p=1.25$ mm, $k=p/(2\pi)$ and
$\epsilon=0.001$ m\,s$^{-1}$. The implemented command set is
\[
\mathcal A_{\rm screw}=
\left\{a:
\begin{array}{l}
|S_v a_v-kS_\omega a_\omega|\leq\epsilon-\eta,\\
|a_v|\leq1,\quad |a_\omega|\leq1,\quad |a_r|\leq r_{\rm int}
\end{array}\right\},
\]
where $\eta=10^{-6}\epsilon$ is the numerical interior offset and
$r_{\rm int}$ the radial bound after the recorded stochastic margin.

In the axial--angular slice, the convex polygon cut from the command square by
the two helical halfspaces, admissible proposals pass unchanged and others move
to the Euclidean-nearest edge point in normalized coordinates; radial action is
clipped independently.

The matched QP minimizes the same objective over the same inequalities;
equal task-level SCREW results do not by themselves certify pointwise numerical
equality. The main-text zero-tolerance line projection only illustrates the
coupling.


\subsection{Disk--interval projection (PCB)}

PCB v2 uses normalized action

\[
a=
\begin{bmatrix}
a_x & a_y & a_z
\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\]

where $a_x,a_y$ command tilt torque and $a_z$ lift. With
declared tilt radius $r_\tau$ (normalized units) and lift
interval $[\ell_z,r_z]$, the admissible set is

\[
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{PCB}}
=
\left\{
a:
a_x^2+a_y^2\leq r_\tau^2,
\;
\ell_z\leq a_z\leq r_z
\right\}.
\]

Writing

\[
\rho=\sqrt{a_x^2+a_y^2},
\]

the exact Euclidean projection of the tilt pair is

\[
(a_x',a_y')
=
\begin{cases}
(a_x,a_y), & \rho\leq r_\tau,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{r_\tau}{\rho}(a_x,a_y), & \rho>r_\tau,
\end{cases}
\]

and the lift component is

\[
a_z'=\min\{r_z,\max\{\ell_z,a_z\}\}.
\]

This projection does \emph{not} zero both tilt torques and is not the submitted
appendix's six-dimensional wrench operator. The physical nominal planarity
limit is $\tau_{\max}=0.100\,\mathrm{N\,m}$; under chance tightening, the
runtime radius derives separately from the margin policy rather than silently
replacing that limit.


\subsection{BATTERY specification and runtime operators}

BATTERY v2 no longer relies solely on the submitted appendix's instantaneous
half-space $F\cdot v\leq P_{\max}(T)$; it distinguishes preventive
deformation limits, modeled damage onset, internal short and subsequent thermal
evolution, so runtime comparisons identify both the \emph{constraint
specification} and the \emph{operator form}. The executable operator
definitions are therefore fingerprinted with the benchmark registry and margin
policy, not restated here as an informal analytical approximation. The
matched-specification experiment compares componentwise and TRiX operators at
the same 15-N preventive threshold; the submitted comparison, which mixed a
20-N flagged peel bound with a different projection geometry, is retained only
as provenance.


\subsection{Phase- and frame-conditioned operators}

In SNAP, pull authority depends on latch state and manipulation phase; in
CRANK, the transverse admissible set also depends on task-frame orientation.
These context-conditioned runtime operators test whether the governor has the
information and action authority the task requires, not whether curved
geometry is generally superior to clipping.


\subsection{PRY restriction}

PRY is a control case in which a simpler restriction can retain
task-relevant authority; a separate fixed-budget control compares the
implemented static and insertion-dependent restrictions, whose similar
completion rates, scoped to those policies and configurations, establish
neither equivalence nor a geometric causal effect.


\section{Supplementary proofs for representation propositions}

This section consolidates the representation results stated in \mainsection{2.1}.
They characterize admissible action sets and are not a closed-loop stability
theorem.

\Needspace*{8\baselineskip}
\subsection*{Proposition 1: separable sets reduce to componentwise clipping}

Let

\[
\mathcal{B}
=
\prod_{i=1}^{m}[\ell_i,r_i]
\]

and consider the Euclidean projection

\[
\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}(\tilde u)
=
\arg\min_{u\in\mathcal{B}}
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(u_i-\tilde u_i)^2 .
\]

Because both the objective and feasible set separate by coordinate, the
optimization decomposes into $m$ scalar problems,

\[
u_i^\star
=
\arg\min_{\ell_i\leq u_i\leq r_i}
\frac{1}{2}(u_i-\tilde u_i)^2 .
\]

Each scalar minimizer is

\[
u_i^\star
=
\min\{r_i,\max\{\ell_i,\tilde u_i\}\},
\]

which is exactly componentwise clipping. Therefore an exact Euclidean
projector has no representational advantage over clipping when both
enforce the same axis-aligned product set with the same margins.

The result does not apply to admissible sets with coupled coordinates, state
dependence, phase dependence, or a different physical margin.


\Needspace*{8\baselineskip}
\subsection*{Proposition 2: consequence of phase blindness}

Let $\mathcal{A}_1$ and $\mathcal{A}_2$ denote the admissible sets in two task
phases and let $\mathcal{B}$ be a single phase-independent restriction.

For $\mathcal{B}$ to be admissible in phase 1,

\[
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_1,
\]

and for it to be admissible in phase 2,

\[
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_2.
\]

Hence

\[
\mathcal{B}
\subseteq
\mathcal{A}_1\cap\mathcal{A}_2 .
\]

If $\mathcal{A}_1\neq\mathcal{A}_2$, their intersection is a strict subset of
at least one phase-specific set. Thus any restriction that is safe in both
phases necessarily removes some phase-specific admissible authority from at
least one phase. Conversely, if a phase-independent restriction admits an
action outside the intersection, that action is inadmissible in at least one
phase.

The proposition establishes only a representation/information limitation,
not that the removed actions are required for task completion; whether the
lost authority is task-essential is determined empirically.


\Needspace*{8\baselineskip}
\subsection*{Proposition 3: conservatism under unknown rotation}

Let

\[
\mathcal{R}_0
=
\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:
|u_1|\leq a,\;
|u_2|\leq b\},
\qquad
a\geq b>0,
\]

and let

\[
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}
=
R(\theta)\mathcal{R}_0 .
\]

First consider any $u$ satisfying $\lVert u\rVert_2\leq b$. Rotation preserves
Euclidean norm, so in every rotated coordinate system each component has
magnitude at most $\lVert u\rVert_2\leq b\leq a$. Hence

\[
u\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta}
\quad
\text{for every }\theta.
\]

Therefore the radius-$b$ disk is contained in
$\bigcap_{\theta}\mathcal{R}_{\theta}$.

Now let $\lVert u\rVert_2>b$. Choose an orientation for which the narrow axis
of the rectangle is aligned with $u$. In that orientation, the narrow-axis
coordinate of $u$ has magnitude $\lVert u\rVert_2>b$, so

\[
u\notin\mathcal{R}_{\theta}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\bigcap_{\theta}\mathcal{R}_{\theta}
=
\{u:\lVert u\rVert_2\leq b\}.
\]

For a symmetric fixed world-frame box $[-h,h]^2$, the corner
$(h,h)$ has norm $\sqrt{2}h$. Requiring every point in the box to remain
inside the radius-$b$ disk gives

\[
\sqrt{2}h\leq b,
\]

and hence

\[
h\leq\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}.
\]

This is a worst-case orientation result. The CRANK experiment exercises
angle-conditioned task-frame constraints but is not interpreted as isolating
rotation alone.


\Needspace*{8\baselineskip}
\subsection*{Proposition 4: feasibility of probabilistic tightening}

Let the nominal scalar physical limit be

\[
|y|\leq L,
\qquad
L>0,
\]

and let uncertainty have standard deviation $\sigma>0$. For a declared
Gaussian quantile $z>0$, the tightening margin is

\[
m=z\sigma.
\]

The tightened admissible interval is

\[
|y|\leq L-z\sigma.
\]

Its radius is therefore

\[
r=L-z\sigma.
\]

Three cases follow directly:

\begin{enumerate}
\item If $z\sigma<L$, then $r>0$ and the tightened set contains nonzero
      admissible commands.
\item If $z\sigma=L$, then $r=0$ and the only admissible command is $y=0$.
\item If $z\sigma>L$, then $r<0$ and the tightened interval is empty.
\end{enumerate}

Thus nonzero command authority exists if and only if

\[
z<\frac{L}{\sigma}.
\]

This separates probabilistic-margin feasibility from projection behavior: no
projection mechanism can recover authority eliminated by the declared margin.


\subsection*{Affine projection and quadratic-program equivalence}

Consider the affine constraint

\[
Au=b
\]

with full-row-rank $A$. The Euclidean projection problem

\[
\min_u
\frac{1}{2}\lVert u-\tilde u\rVert_2^2
\quad
\text{subject to}
\quad
Au=b
\]

has Lagrangian

\[
\mathcal{L}(u,\lambda)
=
\frac{1}{2}\lVert u-\tilde u\rVert_2^2
+
\lambda^\top(Au-b).
\]

Stationarity gives

\[
u-\tilde u+A^\top\lambda=0,
\]

so

\[
u=\tilde u-A^\top\lambda.
\]

Substitution into the equality constraint yields

\[
AA^\top\lambda=A\tilde u-b,
\]

and therefore

\[
u^\star
=
\tilde u
-
A^\top(AA^\top)^{-1}(A\tilde u-b).
\]

A closed-form implementation and a quadratic-program solver given the same
affine constraint therefore solve the same optimization problem. The SCREW set
is instead a polytope (\suppsection{S2.1}), but the same conclusion holds there:
both solvers minimize the same strictly convex objective over the same nonempty
closed convex set, whose minimizer is unique, so the revised SCREW solver
comparison tests numerical agreement and computational cost, not
representational superiority.


\subsection*{Policy adaptation}

No proposition in this section states that training can or cannot overcome a
particular runtime restriction. Filter-aware training changes the policy's
proposal distribution and may let it adapt to retained action authority;
whether that succeeds is task dependent and is reported only as an empirical
result in main Results.

\subsection{Reward and progress contract}

Task-specific rewards replace the submitted PDF's single reconstructed reward
equation. Each environment records a normalized progress quantity for the
frozen checkpoint selection rule:

\begin{itemize}
\item SCREW: axial extraction progress;
\item PCB: lift progress;
\item SNAP: extraction progress;
\item CRANK: rotation progress;
\item BATTERY: peel progress; and
\item PRY: gap-opening progress.
\end{itemize}

Selection ranks safe completion before progress so that a do-nothing policy
cannot win merely by avoiding damage. Rewards affect only learning; the
five-category episode outcome contract is computed independently.

\subsection{Chance-constraint margins}

Stochastic tightening derives each margin from a declared episode-level risk
budget and horizon, allocated across the applicable constraint families. Under
Gaussian noise this is a chance constraint for the assumed distribution, not a
worst-case robustness guarantee. The implementation records whether a
tightened set is

\begin{enumerate}
\item nonempty,
\item nonempty with positive nonzero command authority, or
\item infeasible.
\end{enumerate}

The singleton zero-command set is nonempty yet gives no task-progress authority.

\section{Training configuration}
\subsection{Learning implementation}
The reported policies use PPO~\cite{ref31} and SAC~\cite{ref32}. Earlier
custom SAC and PPO implementations were retired before the reported
experiments and produced no reported result. With a restriction active in
training, the policy emits a \emph{nominal} command and the restriction is
part of the environment transition,
\[
a_{\mathrm{nominal}}\longrightarrow\text{governor}\longrightarrow
a_{\mathrm{executed}}\longrightarrow\text{plant},
\]
so the replay or rollout buffer stores the nominal action
(\emph{filter-as-environment}).

\subsection{Two evaluation modes}
Stage~1 applies each runtime arm to the same frozen nominal policy on the same
held-out episodes; Stage~2 trains a separate policy with its restriction
active, testing adaptation to the retained authority. The stages are reported
separately; neither substitutes for the other.

\subsection{Frozen checkpoint selection and held-out evaluation}
The main Methods selection rule (checkpoints every 1000 steps; 30 screening
and 70 confirmation validation episodes; one evaluation on 100 held-out
episodes per training seed $0,\ldots,9$) was fixed before any reported
held-out evaluation. The scheduler uses deterministic task offsets, not
Python's process-dependent \texttt{hash()}. Historical seed-pairing
mismatches are reported, not overwritten: SCREW training seed~0 was evaluated
on a different held-out block from seeds~1--9, and its
\texttt{oracle\_tangent} arm on a third (S9.5).

\subsection{Recorded configuration}

Table~\ref{tab:submitted-10} records the settings; the wrapper trains a
two-layer $[256,256]$ MLP. Optimizer and update parameters are the pinned
Stable-Baselines3 defaults unless an experiment record overrides them. Budgets
are requested environment steps, which PPO can exceed by finishing rollout
batches: the archived PCB seed-0 PPO models contain 16,384 steps for a
15,000-step request, the SAC models 15,000. Selection uses the recorded

Margins follow the chance-constraint subsection above, with three recorded

\begin{itemize}
\item the tightened set is nonempty;
\item the tightened set retains positive nonzero action authority; or
\item the tightened set is infeasible.
\end{itemize}

BATTERY's componentwise and TRiX arms enforce the same preventive force
threshold, separating specification from projection form; the historical PCB
box and TRiX arms differ in both set form and effective margin, so their
comparison is not an isolated proof of geometric superiority.


\subsection{Reproducibility and provenance}

An implementation audit found that several archived filters did not use the
later per-family margin-policy registry: historical PCB, SNAP, CRANK, BATTERY
and PRY filters used the common default tightening when no multiplier was
supplied, and the SCREW radial restriction used its independently derived task
value. These runs are reported as executed, not regenerated; for them the
registry is declarative metadata, not the complete executable fingerprint.
Comparisons remain margin-matched where both arms use the same bound
construction; PCB and SNAP, whose arms also differ in margin, are
implemented-governor comparisons.

Every quantitative run records its constraint fingerprint, margin-policy,
outcome-taxonomy and protocol-freeze hashes, execution mode, arm, policy
algorithm, training seed, selected checkpoint and episode seeds; records with
missing or mismatched fields are not pooled. The registry is the single
binding between environment versions and runtime arms. The seed schedule,
seed, specification and margin audits, and interpretation limits are in the
source package; frozen historical artifacts are not rewritten after audit
corrections.


\paragraph{Protocol-hash lineage.}

\section{Recorded computational cost}
\label{sec:si-runtime}

The benchmark times the complete governor call (main Methods) for all 20
registered non-passthrough arms across the six tasks, plus the NumPy SCREW
polygon projector and reused OSQP; it replaces the unsupported historical
timing figures without reconstructing their provenance.

\paragraph{Workload and repetition.}
Each task has 2,048 saved float32 observation/action pairs (seed 20260925):
half the actions are uniform in $[-1,1]^3$, a quarter scale the TRiX
projection by a factor in $[0.1,0.9]$, and a quarter move it $10^{-4}$
normalized units toward the proposal before clipping to the action cube.
These strata target different correction costs; their weights are not policy
visitation frequencies. Observations come from the environment encoders,
without rollout or retraining: reset states for state-independent filters,
equal engaged/disengaged SNAP cases, CRANK angles either side of its rotation
threshold and PRY insertion depths over its declared range.

Five fresh Python processes each warm every callable for 256 calls, then time
all 2,048 inputs per implementation (10,240 calls per row, 225,280 overall)
in a seeded permutation, with task and implementation order randomized within
64-input blocks; all arms of a task see the same input sequence. Timing uses
\texttt{perf\_counter\_ns} with cyclic garbage collection disabled, allocation
and reference counting included, and the 0.025~$\mu$s empty timer-pair median
not subtracted. Table~\ref{tab:runtime-latency} reports pooled medians, 95th
percentiles and the range of the five process medians; raw per-call durations
(all outliers), outputs, failure flags, call order, strata and maxima are kept.

\paragraph{Host and solver settings.}
The host was an Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX (Linux 7.0.0-31, CPython 3.12.3,
NumPy 1.26.4, SciPy 1.11.4, quadprog 0.1.13, OSQP 1.1.3), with each worker
pinned to CPU~0, native numerical thread limits and the recorded process
thread count at one, and the \texttt{intel\_pstate} powersave policy and
frequency scaling unchanged. OSQP
reuses its problem object and warm start (absolute and relative tolerances
$10^{-9}$, iteration cap 20,000, polishing with zero refinement iterations);
its object construction (0.662--0.674~ms across the five processes) is
excluded from steady-state times. Active-set QP keeps its per-call matrix
construction, solver diagnostics stay inside the timed call, and worker
output goes to saved logs.

\paragraph{Matched comparison and scope.}
The scalar, NumPy and active-set outputs coincide in float32 and differ from
OSQP by at most $1.013\times10^{-6}$ per action coordinate; all four pass the
predeclared $10^{-5}$ action-agreement and $10^{-7}$ constraint-residual
tolerances with zero recovery outputs, though these saved-bank checks are no
general error bound. The NumPy analytical projector takes 18.16/20.90~$\mu$s
(median/95th percentile), and BATTERY's preventive TRiX variant 3.12~$\mu$s.
Componentwise and oracle-tangent controls implement different restrictions,
so their times are descriptive overheads, not equivalent-safety comparisons.
The largest recorded calls are 352.56~$\mu$s for OSQP and 335.81~$\mu$s for
CRANK TRiX; a single-host distribution establishes no worst-case execution
time or end-to-end real-time guarantee.

\subsection{SNAP: phase-conditioned robust governance versus a static baseline}

SNAP safe completion is 5.2\% (static) and 96.5\% (TRiX) within the training

policy-and-governor configuration without isolating governor, phase
information or tightening as the cause. Nor does it implicate curved
geometry: the phase-conditioned admissible set is axis-aligned, and
phase-aware clipping delegates to the same phase-conditioned projection.


\subsection{CRANK: angle conditioning and task-frame representation vary together}

Filter-aware SAC reaches 0.0\% safe completion on CRANK with the static
restriction and 49.4\% with the task-conditioned governor.
The static arm sets its axial restriction by rotation phase but fixes
transverse bounds in world coordinates at the smaller task-axis authority;
the task-conditioned arm also tracks crank angle and works in the rotating
task frame. The static representation thus omits task-relevant structure,
without isolating angle conditioning, the task-frame transform or both as the
cause, and the training stall is an empirical learning outcome under the
implemented restriction, not proof that a required tangential command is
mathematically excluded at every orientation.


\subsection{PCB and PRY: simpler restrictions can be sufficient after adaptation}

On PCB, filter-aware SAC reaches 100.0\% with componentwise clipping and
99.8\% with TRiX, so exact structured projection shows no material task-level
advantage after adaptation. Stage~1 changed filter form and margin together,
a nominal $0.100$~N\,m per-axis tilt bound against an executed projection
radius of about $0.0871$~N\,m ($12.9\%$ tighter), so it does not isolate set
geometry as the cause.

On PRY it reaches 88.5\% (static) and 89.1\% (TRiX); trained under the
restriction, the policy adapts its proposals to the authority a simpler
governor retains. A separate fixed-budget PRY control (15,000-step SAC, ten
training seeds, 100 evaluation episodes per seed), distinct from the Stage~2
checkpoint-selected means, gives mean safe completion of 0.0\% unrestricted,
56.0\% static and 59.2\% TRiX, with six of ten seeds reaching at least 50\% in
each restricted arm. The restrictions share the intended zero-insertion
bounds but differ as insertion proceeds; the archive's provenance note reports
an initial volume check without its sample record, so no numerical volume
estimate is retained. Both restrictions are useful here, without proven
equivalence or a causal role for task-dependent geometry.

policies (ten SAC and ten PPO training seeds) without retraining, testing
whether the metric selecting the nearest admissible command changes outcomes
when the admissible set is fixed. In production normalized coordinates
$(a_v,a_\omega)$, TRiX projects onto the unchanged admissible polygon with
\[
d_\kappa(u,\tilde u)^2=\kappa^{-2}(a_v-\tilde a_v)^2+(a_\omega-\tilde a_\omega)^2,
\]
that is, Euclidean projection with axial scale $\kappa S_v$ in place of $S_v$,
while the radial command keeps its production margin-tightened bound. Any
positive weighting returns a point of the same polygon, so $\kappa$ changes
only the selected correction, not admissibility. The declared scales,
$S_v=0.05$~m\,s$^{-1}$ and $S_\omega=60$~rad\,s$^{-1}$, were fixed in
the task code before any policy was trained and are unchanged since.

Two gates precede comparison: at $\kappa=1$ the reconstructed projector
matches the production scalar TRiX projector within the declared $10^{-6}$
tolerance on 10,000 uniformly sampled commands, and the replay reproduces
every archived TRiX safe-completion count for all 20 policies, seed for seed.
Policies replay their archived held-out Stage~1 test-shard episodes
(879000--879099 for training seed~0 of both algorithms, 899000--899099 for
seeds~1--9), shared by TRiX and the matched QP for every seed; the archived

\section{Representation and prospective generalization studies}
\subsection{Procedural admissible-set benchmark}

To test representation apart from the six disassembly environments, we
generate three-dimensional admissible sets from six families:

\[
\{\text{box},\text{ ellipsoid},\text{ polytope},
  \text{ state-scaled},\text{ rotating},\text{ phase-gated}\}.
\]

The generator is frozen as specification v1.3; separate random streams produce
120 training, 40 validation and 200 held-out instances. Each instance has a
required action, sampled from the interior of its exact admissible set, standing
in for task-essential control authority.

Learned models are fitted separately on each evaluated geometry from 3,000
labeled state--action samples (250 fitting steps for the nonlinear model), so
``held-out'' refers to the geometry bank, not to transfer of one learned model.
Retention permits displacement below
$0.02(\lVert u_{\rm req}\rVert+10^{-9})$. The context-free box uses the
implemented inscribed-box construction and, for context-dependent families,
the componentwise minimum across 21 sampled contexts; we claim no global
maximal-volume or continuous-context certification for it.


\subsection{Learned representation failure is not explained by training-set size}

The PCB decomposition evaluates representations of the admissible set, not
complete trained safety algorithms. On the held-out proposals, componentwise
clipping yields 2.9\% feasible commands, a learned action-affine representation
0.0\%, and a learned nonlinear representation 78.0\% with one correction step
and 92.3\% iterated; the per-action analytical oracle and exact structured
projection each reach 100\%.

The action-affine result stays at 0.0\% as its training set grows from 500 to
20,000 samples, a representational limitation of that model class in this
experiment rather than insufficient data. The iterated nonlinear result
counters a simple ``learned versus symbolic'' reading, but it is a
proposal-feasibility rate, not set recovery; exact projection differs because
it is given the declared set rather than learning an approximation. The
archived run uses seed 0, 20,000 fitting samples and 2,000 test proposals, so
the gain from 78.0\% to 92.3\% is not established across seeds.


\subsection{Procedural benchmark separates context, approximation and exact projection}

The partition of the 200 held-out instances, fixed before evaluation, is a
constructed calibration test: the ``needs structure'' group is defined by the
implemented context-free axis-aligned box excluding the required action, so
that box's failure there is close to definitional, not independent validation
of the criterion. The empirical
information is how other representations behave inside each group.

\subsection{Prospective BAYONET stress test}

BAYONET was preregistered to test whether the representation criterion could
predict prospectively rather than only explain the original six tasks. Static
and phase-gated variants, comparator arms and a competence gate were frozen
before the learned-policy runs. SAC was the primary confirmatory algorithm and
PPO a replication, with a 15,000-step training budget per seed, the same frozen
checkpoint-selection rule, ten training seeds and 100 held-out episodes. A
variant with no learned arm reaching 50\% completion was declared
\emph{uninformative} in advance.


\subsection{The prospective BAYONET test was inconclusive}

The gate was not met: every arm stayed below 50\%, with a highest ten-seed
mean safe-completion rate of 10.0\% (individual seeds are not bounded by that
mean), so the test neither confirms nor falsifies the criterion. Gate and task
were frozen before the final comparison and not softened afterward; reporting
this null result is part of the evidence that the criterion was not repeatedly
modified until every new experiment agreed with it.

\paragraph{Visual tilt scaling and margin strata.}
The bank generator stores a display angle $\phi$ and a normalized parameter
$t=\texttt{tilt\_norm}$. For the 21 main-strata configurations, before
rounding,
\[
b=\texttt{TAU\_ROBUST}(1-\texttt{INTERIOR}),\qquad
m=1-|t|/b,\qquad \phi=0.9t/b.
\]
Here $b\simeq0.174174$ is the normalized robust tilt-command bound and $m$
the generator's relative margin. Replay restores the mechanical curvature
angle
\[
\theta_x=tS_\tau/K_{\rm bend}=0.25t,\qquad \theta_y=0.
\]
The displayed angle is thus approximately $20.669\theta_x$ in the main strata.
The seven partial configurations use $t=\phi/0.9$, giving $\phi=3.6\theta_x$, and have no
margin stratum. For example, \texttt{s00\_normal.png} stores $\phi=0.3248$ rad
and $t=0.0629$, while replay restores $\theta_x=0.015725$ rad; both use the
recorded lift of 0.00797 m.

The display is a visibility-scaled cue, not a realizable intact-board state
under the benchmark's bending model: near-boundary
$|\theta_x|=0.0358$--$0.042525$ rad displays as
$|\phi|=42.4^\circ$--$50.4^\circ$, beyond the $0.075$ rad ($4.30^\circ$)
fracture threshold if read as mechanical curvature. In the main strata,
positive scaling preserves tilt sign and the ordering of tilt magnitude and
margin, which the stored values retain, but not the physical angle, so
apparent image distance to a boundary is not a physical safety margin. The same
mechanical tilt displays about 5.74-fold larger in a main-stratum image than
in a partial view. Byte-identical re-rendering from saved display parameters
tests rendering reproducibility, not equality of displayed and mechanical
tilt. The VLM bank is separate from the six manuscript workspace illustrations.

The archive calls the admissible category ``executable''; admissible and
inadmissible grounded commands are both replayed, without human annotation of
semantic correctness. The historical \texttt{projection\_prevented\_harm}
field combines violations with destructive completion. Both arms record zero
destructive completions and zero mechanical failures in every VLM replay, so
the field, like the main Methods' endpoint, measures prevented constraint
violation, not damage or fracture. This is a single-proposal study,
not an autonomous agent loop.

\section{Reactive-governance failure mechanisms}
\subsection{Thermal latency: a current command cannot remove accumulated state}

BATTERY v2 models latent internal-short dynamics that keep generating heat
after external force is removed. Its acceptance test applies a damaging
command, zeroes it for 2,000 steps and checks that heating continues, a property of the
declared model, not independent thermal validation. Trajectory temperatures are omitted,
as their supporting trace was not recovered in the revision archive.

A governor acting only on current commands cannot remove energy already in
that state, so prevention must precede it. With the same
15-N preventive threshold, the componentwise arm and TRiX both reach 100\% safe
completion; the earlier difference between filter forms reflected different
physical thresholds, not the projection operator.


\subsection{Sequential constraints: projection cannot invent a missing prerequisite}

In SNAP, safe extraction requires latch deflection before pulling. A reactive
governor can suppress a pull that is inadmissible in the current latch state
but cannot create the missing deflection; that requires an upstream planner, temporal specification or other
precondition-creating mechanism.


\subsection{Probabilistic tightening can eliminate command authority}

For CRANK, with axial force-noise standard deviation 1.235 N and nominal
limit 5.00 N, the largest feasible Gaussian multiplier is approximately

\[
z_{\max}=\frac{5.00}{1.235}\approx4.05.
\]

The declared episode-level allowance requires $z=4.488$, so the tightened
limit is negative and no positive command authority remains. The noise model is not relaxed; the nominal 5-N CRANK
configuration is excluded from enforcement-guarantee claims, and its separate
representation study, which does not require this margin, is interpreted on
that narrower basis.
